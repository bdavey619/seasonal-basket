August · San Diego

Cherry tomatoes

Cherry tomatoes are still running strong in August and can be exceptional — concentrated flavor, thin skins that burst easily. Where July favored big heirlooms for slicing and eating raw, August is when cherry tomatoes hit their stride, especially the sungold and mixed varieties.

How to choose it

  • Firm but not hard — should give slightly when pressed
  • Deep, even color throughout (red, orange, or yellow depending on variety)
  • Fragrant at the stem end
  • Avoid any with wrinkled skins or soft spots

Buy this much: 1 pint for the week — they'll appear in pasta, tacos, and rice bowls, and disappear faster than expected.

Cherry tomatoes are widely available in August at most grocery stores and farmers markets. Mixed varieties at farmers markets often include sungolds and grape tomatoes, which are worth seeking out.

During the week

  • Burst in olive oil and tossed with pasta
  • Halved raw in tacos or rice bowls
  • Eaten as a snack, straight from the container

On a weekend

Roasted slowly at lower heat until they collapse and concentrate. Spoon over burrata or add to a grain salad.

One thing worth learning

Bursting cherry tomatoes in a hot pan is one of the most reliable weekday techniques. The key is patience — add them to a hot pan with olive oil, don't stir, and let them blister and split on their own. The skin will tighten, then burst, releasing liquid that becomes the sauce. Stir once they've started to collapse. Add garlic and basil after the heat is off.

Good with this month

Basil, Bell peppers, Eggplant, Zucchini, Jalapeños

Good with your staples

pasta, chicken thighs, white beans, eggs

Storage

Counter at room temperature, away from direct sunlight. Do not refrigerate — cold ruins the texture and flavor. Use within three or four days.
