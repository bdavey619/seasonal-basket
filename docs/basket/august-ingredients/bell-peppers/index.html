August · San Diego

Bell peppers

Red and yellow bell peppers are fully ripened green peppers — and August is when they arrive in abundance. The color change takes weeks on the plant, which is why they cost more and why they taste completely different. The bitterness is gone. What's left is sweet, thick, and worth roasting.

How to choose it

  • Fully red or fully yellow — no green patches
  • Firm and heavy for their size
  • Glossy skin without soft spots
  • The thicker the walls, the better for roasting

Buy this much: 3–4 peppers for the week. Two go into the house flavor; the rest go into meals.

Red and yellow peppers cost more than green because they've spent longer on the plant. In August, supply increases and prices tend to soften slightly.

During the week

  • Sliced raw into tacos or rice bowls
  • Roasted and added to a rice bowl or pasta
  • Used as the base for the charred pepper sauce

On a weekend

Roasted whole alongside chicken thighs. Tear them open over the plate.

One thing worth learning

A fully red bell pepper and a green bell pepper are the same vegetable at different points of ripeness. The green one was picked early. Weeks more on the plant produce the color, the sweetness, and the complete absence of that grassy bitterness. This is also why charring works so well on red peppers — there's actual sugar to caramelize.

Good with this month

Eggplant, Cherry tomatoes, Jalapeños, Basil, Zucchini

Good with your staples

chicken thighs, salmon, eggs, white beans

Storage

Refrigerator crisper. Up to a week before roasting; a few days once roasted.

  • Charred Pepper Sauce
