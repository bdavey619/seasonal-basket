November · San Diego

Fuyu persimmons

Persimmons ripen on bare trees in November, and San Diego County grows them. Fuyus are the squat, tomato-shaped kind you can eat crisp like an apple — sweet, a little like honey and pumpkin.

How to choose it

  • Squat and flat-bottomed: that's a Fuyu, ready to eat firm
  • Deep orange all over, with no yellow-green patches
  • Firm but not rock-hard for salads; a little give for eating out of hand
  • Skip pointed, acorn-shaped ones unless they're jelly-soft: those are Hachiya

Buy this much: 4 for mornings and snacks; 2 more if you make the halibut.

Sold by the each. Farmers markets often have them in bags for less.

During the week

  • Shaved into the salad beside seared halibut
  • Sliced over yogurt in the morning
  • With Parmesan and pepper, as a snack

On a weekend

On buttered toast with flaky salt, for a slow breakfast.

One thing worth learning

Shape tells you how to eat a persimmon. A squat Fuyu is never astringent and can be eaten crisp. A pointed Hachiya is full of tannin until it's completely soft, like a water balloon; bite one early and your mouth goes dry.

Good with this month

Fennel, Satsuma mandarins, Cauliflower

Good with your staples

Greek yogurt, sourdough

Storage

On the counter until they're as soft as you like, then the fridge for up to a week.
