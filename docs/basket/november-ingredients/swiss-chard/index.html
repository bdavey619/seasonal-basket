Swiss chard

Chard is one of the greens that does best in Southern California's cool season. It's tender enough to cook in minutes and sturdy enough for a pot of soup.

How to choose it

  • Glossy, upright leaves with no yellowing
  • Crisp stems that snap rather than bend
  • Any color — rainbow, red, or white — cooks the same

Buy this much: 3 bunches for the suggested week: the soup, the orecchiette, and the farro bowls (and a frittata).

Sold by the bunch.

During the week

  • Stirred into the beef and white bean soup
  • Wilted through orecchiette with sausage and leeks
  • Wilted into the braise juices for the farro bowls

On a weekend

Stems and leaves folded into a chard and Parmesan frittata.

One thing worth learning

Chard is two vegetables. The stems need five minutes longer than the leaves, so chop them and start them first; add the leaves at the end, when they only need to wilt.

Good with this month

Leeks, Cauliflower

Good with your staples

eggs, beans

Storage

In the fridge, wrapped loosely in a damp towel in a bag, for up to five days.
