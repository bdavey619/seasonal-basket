Satsuma mandarins

Satsumas are among the first California citrus of the season, and they start arriving in November — sweet, seedless, and so loose in their skins they peel in one piece. They're at their best now and through December.

How to choose it

  • Heavy for their size; light ones have started to dry out
  • A puffy, loose peel is normal for a satsuma, not a sign of age
  • Bright orange, with a little green near the stem still fine
  • No soft, sunken spots

Buy this much: A 2-lb bag, about ten. One goes into the jar and the braise; the rest get eaten out of hand.

Usually sold in bags or by the pound; the bags are cheaper and just as good.

During the week

  • Peel into the pickled fennel
  • Zest over yogurt and persimmon
  • Segments over the roast chicken and cauliflower

On a weekend

Juice and zest into the weekend braise, where they keep a rich pot bright.

One thing worth learning

A satsuma's loose peel is the variety, not age. Judge them by weight instead: a heavy one is full of juice.

Good with this month

Fennel, Fuyu persimmons, Leeks

Good with your staples

Greek yogurt, chicken thighs

Storage

In the fridge, loose, for up to two weeks. On the counter they're fine for a few days.

House Flavor

  • Pickled fennel
