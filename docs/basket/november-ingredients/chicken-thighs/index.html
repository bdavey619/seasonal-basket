Bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs

There's nothing seasonal about chicken. What changes in November is the weather: the first cool evenings make an hour-long braise appealing again, and bone-in, skin-on thighs are the cut built for it. This is the month's cut.

How to choose it

  • Skin that covers the whole thigh, not shrunken back
  • Pink, plump meat with no gray edges
  • No liquid pooling in the package
  • Similar-sized thighs, so they cook evenly

Buy this much: 6, about 2½–3 lbs: the braise, with four left for the farro bowls. Four more if you make the roast chicken and cauliflower.

Bone-in, skin-on thighs are one of the cheapest cuts in the case, well below boneless.

During the week

  • Leftover braise, pulled off the bone over farro
  • Roasted with cauliflower and pickled fennel

On a weekend

Seared and braised in the Dutch oven with leeks, fennel, and satsuma. The weekend dish.

One thing worth learning

Thighs are done at 165°F but better at 175–195°F: dark meat has connective tissue that keeps softening as it cooks, so a braised thigh at 195°F is more tender than one pulled at 165°F, not less.

Good with this month

Leeks, Fennel, Satsuma mandarins, Cauliflower

Good with your staples

rice, sourdough

Storage

In the coldest part of the fridge, in the package, for a day or two. Cook within two days of buying.
