Fennel

Fennel is a cool-weather vegetable; as the nights drop into the fifties it comes back sweet and crisp. Raw, it's anise-cool and crunchy; cooked slowly, it turns soft and mild.

How to choose it

  • Heavy, tight, white bulbs
  • No brown bruising or splitting on the outer layers
  • Fresh, upright fronds; limp fronds mean it's been sitting

Buy this much: 3 bulbs: two for the jar, one for the braise. One more if you make the halibut.

Sold by the bulb. Bigger bulbs are usually the better deal.

During the week

  • Pickled, over lentils, beside steak, and on the farro bowls
  • Shaved raw with persimmon beside seared halibut

On a weekend

Cut in wedges and braised with leeks under the chicken thighs.

One thing worth learning

Fennel changes completely with heat: raw it's crisp and cool, cooked it goes soft and sweet and loses most of its anise. Someone who doesn't like it raw may love it braised.

Good with this month

Satsuma mandarins, Fuyu persimmons, Leeks

Good with your staples

chicken thighs, rice

Storage

In the fridge, in a bag, for about a week. Cut off the stalks if they don't fit; keep a few fronds.

House Flavor

  • Pickled fennel
