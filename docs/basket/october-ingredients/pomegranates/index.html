Pomegranates

Southern California grows pomegranates, and they ripen in October and November. A heavy one bought now keeps for weeks and turns anything rich into something brighter.

How to choose it

  • Heavy for its size — weight is juice
  • Slightly angular, flat-sided shapes are the fullest
  • Taut, leathery skin
  • A dry crack is fine; a soft or wet one is not

Buy this much: 2. One for the drink, one for scattering. One pomegranate is about a cup of seeds.

Priced by the piece, so the heavy ones are the deal.

During the week

  • Over roasted delicata, while it's still hot
  • On a rice bowl with sweet potato and salsa macha
  • On sweet potato tacos
  • Over yogurt with honey

On a weekend

Pressed into the South Park Shrub, with a spoonful of seeds saved for the glass.

One thing worth learning

Pomegranate is October's lime: something sharp to put against heavier food. Rich things need acid — lemon in the braise, cider vinegar in the shrub, pomegranate on the squash. Same move, different ingredient.

Good with this month

Delicata squash, Sweet potatoes, Late Julian apples, Fuyu persimmons

Good with your staples

Greek yogurt, ground beef or turkey, sticky white rice

Storage

Whole, a month in the fridge. Seeded, five days in a covered container.
