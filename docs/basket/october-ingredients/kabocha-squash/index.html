Kabocha squash

Kabocha is the squash that comes after the thin-skinned early ones — dense, dry, and sweeter than butternut. It's picked now and actually improves after a few weeks on the counter, which makes it the first thing in the year you can buy in October and cook in November.

How to choose it

  • Dark green, dull rather than shiny — shine means it was picked early
  • Heavy enough to surprise you
  • A dry, corky stem
  • A pale orange patch on one side is fine; that's where it sat on the ground
  • No soft spots anywhere

Buy this much: 1 large, about 3 lbs. That's two trays of roasted wedges — most of a week of bowls and tacos.

Usually sold by the pound. Cheaper at Asian and Mexican markets than at most supermarkets.

During the week

  • Roasted wedges over rice with ajvar
  • Cubed in tacos with black beans and cotija
  • Next to salmon on the same sheet pan
  • Cold, straight from the fridge, with salt

On a weekend

A tray of dark-roasted wedges on the table beside the whole fish, for anyone who wants something warm next to the salad.

One thing worth learning

Kabocha is drier than other squash, which is why it roasts so well: there's less water to steam off before the sugars can brown. That's also why it can taste chalky if undercooked. Roast it until a knife slides in with no resistance at all, then give it five more minutes.

Good with this month

Pomegranates, Arugula, Red bell peppers, New-crop dates

Good with your staples

sticky white rice, beans, Greek yogurt, chicken thighs, salmon

Storage

Whole, somewhere cool and dry for up to two months — it gets sweeter over the first few weeks. Once cut, wrap it and refrigerate; use within five days.
