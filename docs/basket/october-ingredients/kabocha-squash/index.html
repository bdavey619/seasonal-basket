Kabocha squash

Kabocha is the squash that comes after the thin-skinned early ones — dense, dry, and sweeter than butternut. It's picked now and actually improves after a few weeks on the counter, which makes it the first thing in the year you can buy in October and cook in November.

How to choose it

  • Dark green, dull rather than shiny — shine means it was picked early
  • Heavy enough to surprise you
  • A dry, corky stem
  • A pale orange patch on one side is fine; that's where it sat on the ground
  • No soft spots anywhere

Buy this much: 1 large, about 3 lbs. Half goes to the weekend, half to the week.

Sold by the pound. Usually the cheapest squash per serving in the store, because almost none of it is water.

During the week

  • On the sheet pan with chicken sausage and peppers
  • On the warm plate with ground lamb and yogurt
  • Cold from the fridge, with salt and a squeeze of lemon
  • Leftover wedges, cubed into the skillet hash

On a weekend

Roasted in wedges, under the seared rockfish and brown butter.

One thing worth learning

Kabocha is drier than other squash, which is why it roasts so well: there's less water to steam off before the sugars can brown. It's also why it can taste chalky if undercooked. Roast it until a knife slides in with no resistance at all, then give it five more minutes.

Good with this month

Pomegranates, Arugula, Red bell peppers, Medjool dates, Rockfish fillets

Good with your staples

rice, Greek yogurt, black beans

Storage

Whole, somewhere cool and dry for up to two months — it gets sweeter over the first few weeks. Once cut, wrap it and refrigerate; use within five days.
