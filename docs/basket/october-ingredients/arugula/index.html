October · San Diego

Arugula

Arugula is a cool-weather green. In summer heat it bolts and turns harsh; planted in September, it's ready in October with a gentler, peppery bite. It's the first green in months that tastes like it wanted to grow.

How to choose it

  • Small leaves are milder than big, deeply lobed ones
  • Perky leaves with no yellowing
  • No wet or slimy leaves at the bottom of the bag or bunch
  • Bunches usually taste more of themselves than washed clamshells

Buy this much: 2 bunches, or one big bag. It doesn't keep like the rest of the basket — use it first.

Bunches are often cheaper per ounce than clamshells.

During the week

  • On the warm plate over roasted squash and rice
  • In fish tacos in place of lettuce
  • On top of toast with ajvar and an egg
  • Beside a baked sweet potato, dressed with lemon

On a weekend

The base of the October salad: arugula, pomegranate, torn dates, and feta, dressed at the last second.

One thing worth learning

Dress greens at the last possible moment. Salt pulls water out of leaves, and oil weighs them down, so a salad dressed ten minutes early is a wilted salad. Put everything in the bowl, carry it to the table, and toss it there.

Good with this month

Pomegranates, Kabocha squash, Sweet potatoes, Red bell peppers, Medjool dates, Rockfish fillets

Good with your staples

rice, eggs, sourdough, black beans

Storage

Unwashed, wrapped in a dry paper towel inside a bag, in the crisper. Three or four days. Wash it right before using.
