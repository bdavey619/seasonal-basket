October · San Diego

Sweet potatoes

California's sweet potatoes are dug in September and October, most of them in the Central Valley around Merced, then stored through the winter. The new crop is on shelves now. It's the basket's filling one: a starch that keeps for weeks and makes a dinner on its own.

How to choose it

  • Firm from end to end, with no soft or wet tips
  • Smooth skin, no sprouts or wrinkling
  • Medium and evenly shaped, so they cook at one speed
  • Orange-fleshed (garnet or jewel) for roasting

Buy this much: 4 medium. Two baked dinners for two, or one dinner and a sheet pan.

Cheap now, and cheaper loose by the pound than in bags.

During the week

  • Baked whole, split, with salted yogurt, black beans, and ajvar
  • Cubed on the sheet pan instead of kabocha
  • Under a fried egg, with arugula
  • Leftover, cold, with a squeeze of lime

On a weekend

Bake two extra while the oven's on for the weekend meal. They're Monday's dinner.

One thing worth learning

A baked sweet potato is done well after it's merely soft. Keep going — an hour at 400°F — until the skin puffs away from the flesh and syrup starts to bead out and caramelize on the pan. That's the starch turning to sugar, and it's the difference between a side and a meal.

Good with this month

Red bell peppers, Pomegranates, Arugula, Kabocha squash

Good with your staples

black beans, Greek yogurt, eggs

Storage

Somewhere cool and dark, not the fridge — cold makes the centers hard and off-tasting. They keep three weeks or more.
