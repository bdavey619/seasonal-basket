October · San Diego

Guavas

Guavas ripen in San Diego backyards from late summer into winter, and October is when they fill the bins at Mexican markets and farm stands. Pink-fleshed and fragrant, they're the most tropical thing in a fall basket — a reminder that fall here isn't New England.

How to choose it

  • Smell first: a ripe guava is fragrant from a foot away
  • Pale yellow-green skin, turning from hard green
  • A slight give when pressed, like a ripe pear
  • Small brown marks are fine; soft, wet spots are not

Buy this much: 5–6. Some ripe now, some firmer for later in the week.

Much cheaper at Mexican markets, where they're sold as guayaba.

During the week

  • Sliced with lime, salt, and Tajín in the afternoon
  • With queso fresco and a tortilla
  • Over yogurt with pomegranate
  • A slice in the drink

On a weekend

Sliced on a plate with queso fresco and new dates, after the fish.

One thing worth learning

You eat all of a guava: the skin, the flesh, and the small hard seeds. Slice it into rounds and don't bother digging anything out. If the seeds bother you, halve it and scoop the smooth outer flesh, leaving the seedy center for the drink.

Good with this month

Pomegranates, Asian pears, New-crop dates

Good with your staples

Greek yogurt, sourdough

Storage

On the counter until they're fragrant and give slightly, then in the fridge for three or four days. Their smell travels — keep them in a closed bag.
