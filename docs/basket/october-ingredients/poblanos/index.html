October · San Diego

The last poblanos

Roasting chiles run from late August to about mid-October, then they're gone until next year. The ones still around this month are often blushing red — a little sweeter, sometimes a little hotter. Left longer, they become anchos, which is where this month's jar starts.

How to choose it

  • Glossy and heavy, with no wrinkles
  • Flat-sided ones char more evenly
  • A red blush means riper — sweeter, and sometimes hotter
  • Skip anything soft at the shoulders

Buy this much: 4–6. Two go into the braise raw. Char the rest and freeze the strips for November.

Still cheap for the first two weeks. After that, what's left is whatever didn't sell.

During the week

  • Charred strips in a rice bowl with sweet potato
  • In tacos with sweet potato and salsa macha
  • Chopped into scrambled eggs
  • Frozen in strips for November

On a weekend

Sliced raw into the Dutch oven with garlic and rosemary, as the aromatic under the weekend braise.

One thing worth learning

Raw poblano and charred poblano are two different vegetables. Sliced raw and cooked in fat, it's green and grassy, doing what a bell pepper does with more to say. Charred, it's smoky and soft. The braise wants the first; the rice bowl wants the second.

Good with this month

Delicata squash, Sweet potatoes, Pomegranates

Good with your staples

chicken thighs, ground beef or turkey, beans, eggs, sticky white rice

Storage

Raw, a week in the crisper. Charred and peeled, four days in the fridge — or freeze the strips flat in a bag and break off what you need.

House Flavor

  • Salsa macha
