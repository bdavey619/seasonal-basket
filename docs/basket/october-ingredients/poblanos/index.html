The last poblanos

Roasting chiles run from late August to about mid-October, then they're gone until next year. The ones still around this month are often blushing red, a little sweeter and sometimes a little hotter. Buy them now; they freeze better than almost anything.

The default move

Char it. You already know how.

Blacken over a flame → cover 15 minutes → slip off the skin → tear into strips

How to choose it

  • Glossy and heavy, with no wrinkles
  • Flat-sided ones char more evenly
  • A red blush means riper — sweeter, and sometimes hotter

Buy this much: 4–6. Two go into the braise raw. Char the rest and freeze the strips for November.

During the week

  • Sliced raw into the braise with the garlic
  • Charred strips in a ground-beef rice bowl
  • In tacos with roasted sweet potato
  • Chopped into scrambled eggs

One thing worth learning

Raw poblano and charred poblano are two different vegetables. Sliced raw and cooked in fat, it's green and grassy, doing what a bell pepper does with more to say. Charred, it's smoky and soft. The braise wants the first. The rice bowl wants the second.

Good with this month

Delicata squash, Sweet potatoes, Pomegranates

Cook it with

Bone-in chicken thighs, California spiny lobster

→ Pairings

Good with your staples

ground beef or turkey, chicken thighs, beans, eggs, sticky white rice

Storage

Raw, a week in the crisper. Charred and peeled, four days in the fridge — or freeze the strips flat in a bag and break off what you need.
