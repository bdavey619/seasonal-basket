October · San Diego

Red bell peppers

A red pepper is a green pepper left on the plant long enough to ripen, and by the end of the season that's most of what's left. Early fall is when California's red peppers are sweetest and often cheapest. By November they'll be shipped from farther away and priced like it.

How to choose it

  • Glossy, firm, and heavy for their size
  • Deep, even red, with no green shoulders
  • Thick walls — press gently; they shouldn't flex
  • A green, fresh-looking stem
  • Blocky, four-lobed peppers char more evenly than twisted ones

Buy this much: 6. Four for the jar, two for the sheet pan.

Loose peppers by the pound are almost always cheaper than the packaged bags.

During the week

  • Charred and cooked down into the ajvar
  • In strips on the sheet pan with sausage and squash
  • Sliced raw, with salt, while dinner cooks
  • Diced into the skillet hash

On a weekend

Charred on Saturday, cooked down into a jar of ajvar that carries the week.

One thing worth learning

Green, yellow, and red bell peppers are mostly the same plant at different stages. Red ones have spent the longest ripening, which is why they're sweetest and cost more most of the year. The end of the season is when that extra time is cheap.

Good with this month

Kabocha squash, Sweet potatoes, Arugula, Rockfish fillets

Good with your staples

eggs, black beans, rice

Storage

Unwashed in the crisper for a week to ten days. Charred and peeled, four days in the fridge.

House Flavor

  • Ajvar
