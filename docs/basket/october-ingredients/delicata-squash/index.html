Delicata squash

September brought the first ones. By October they've cured longer — more orange down in the grooves, sweeter inside — and they cost less. It's the squash in the Sunday braise, and the one worth keeping two of on the counter all month.

The default move

Don't peel it.

Halve → seed → slice → olive oil + salt → roast at 425°F

How to choose it

  • Orange in the grooves between the green stripes means longer cured and sweeter
  • Heavy for its size and firm at both ends
  • A dry, intact stem
  • Small to medium; the big ones get watery

Buy this much: 3. Two for roasting through the week, one for the pot on Sunday.

During the week

  • Roasted beside salmon on the same sheet pan
  • Folded into pasta with butter browned with sage
  • Cold from the fridge in a rice bowl, with pomegranate
  • In the Dutch oven with chicken and white beans

One thing worth learning

Delicata holds its shape in a braise where butternut would collapse. The thin skin you don't peel is structure. For the pot, cut it thick — an inch — so it turns tender when the chicken does, not before.

Good with this month

Late Julian apples, Pomegranates, The last poblanos, Sweet potatoes

Cook it with

Bone-in chicken thighs, White beans, Rosemary and sage, Lemon

→ Pairings

Good with your staples

salmon, chicken thighs, beans, sticky white rice, pasta

Storage

On the counter, out of the sun, for a month. It doesn't want the fridge. Once cut, wrap it and use it within four days.
