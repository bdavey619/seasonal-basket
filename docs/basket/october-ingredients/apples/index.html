October · San Diego

Late Julian apples

September's apples were for eating raw. October's are Julian's late ones — Granny Smith, Pink Lady, Winesap — tarter and firmer, which is exactly what you want in a hot pan. They hold their shape next to pork instead of turning to sauce.

How to choose it

  • Firm, with no give near the stem
  • Tart varieties for the pan; sweeter ones for eating
  • The blossom end should smell like apple
  • Taut skin, not waxy
  • Ask which ones hold up to heat

Buy this much: 4–6. Two for the pan, the rest for eating. Buy them weekly; crisp is the point.

Julian's orchards thin out by the end of the month. Buy them early if you're driving up.

During the week

  • In the pan after pork chops, with sage
  • Sliced thin into a slaw with pomegranate
  • Grated over yogurt with honey
  • With sharp cheddar and sourdough

On a weekend

Thick wedges browned in butter and served warm with a spoonful of yogurt — the only dessert October needs.

One thing worth learning

A cooking apple is just a tart, firm one. Sugar makes an apple collapse under heat; acid and density hold it together. That's why September said don't bake them yet, and October says go ahead.

Good with this month

Delicata squash, Sweet potatoes, Pomegranates, Fuyu persimmons

Good with your staples

Greek yogurt, sourdough, chicken thighs

Storage

In the fridge, away from anything that absorbs smells. Cold, they stay crisp for weeks.
