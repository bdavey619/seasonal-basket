October · San Diego

Whole rockfish

Rockfish is the local fish that gets more reliable as the water cools in fall: dozens of species, all mild and firm, and usually cheaper than anything flown in. Bought whole, it's the easiest good fish dinner there is — and the one most people have never cooked. This is the month to swap it in for salmon.

How to choose it

  • Clear, bright, slightly bulging eyes; cloudy or sunken means it's been sitting
  • Bright red gills under the flap; brown means old
  • Firm flesh that springs back when pressed
  • It should smell like the ocean, not like fish
  • Ask the counter to scale and gut it — they will, and it takes them two minutes

Buy this much: One fish, 2–3 lbs, feeds two or three. For a weeknight, 1 lb of fillets feeds two.

Usually $6–10 a pound whole, less than salmon fillets. Asian markets and the harbor's weekend fish market sell it whole; supermarkets more often sell fillets, sometimes labeled rock cod or Pacific snapper.

During the week

  • Fillets pan-seared, over rice, with a spoonful of ajvar
  • Flaked into tacos with arugula and pomegranate
  • Leftovers from the whole fish, cold, with lemon and olive oil on toast
  • In place of salmon in anything you already make

On a weekend

Roasted whole at 450°F with lemon, beside the October salad. The weekend meal.

One thing worth learning

A whole fish is more forgiving than a fillet. The bone slows the heat and the skin holds in moisture, so the window between done and overdone is minutes, not seconds. To check, push a thin knife into the thickest part near the spine: if the flesh is opaque and pulls away from the bone, it's done.

Good with this month

Arugula, Pomegranates, Red bell peppers, Sweet potatoes

Good with your staples

corn tortillas, sticky white rice, black beans, sourdough

Storage

Cook it the day you buy it, or the next. Keep it in the coldest part of the fridge on a plate, loosely covered, ideally over a bag of ice.
