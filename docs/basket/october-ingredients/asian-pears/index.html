Asian pears

California's Asian pear harvest runs from August into October, and the late varieties are the best of it. They're picked ripe and eaten crisp — unlike European pears, you don't wait for them to soften. They keep for weeks, which makes them October's lunchbox fruit.

How to choose it

  • Firm all over; this pear is supposed to be hard
  • Heavy, which means juice
  • Fragrant at the stem end
  • Russeted brown or smooth yellow, depending on the variety — both are good
  • No soft spots; they bruise easily, which is why they come in foam sleeves

Buy this much: 3–4. Enough for the salad and a few afternoons.

Expensive in a supermarket, often half that at Asian markets in Convoy.

During the week

  • Sliced cold, in the afternoon
  • Thin slices in the arugula salad
  • With sharp cheese and walnuts
  • Diced over yogurt

On a weekend

Sliced thin into the October salad, beside the whole fish.

One thing worth learning

Some fruit is ripe when it softens, and some is ripe when it's picked. A European pear finishes ripening on your counter; an Asian pear is done when it comes off the tree, and waiting only makes it mealy. Knowing which is which saves a lot of fruit.

Good with this month

Arugula, New walnuts, Pomegranates, New-crop dates

Good with your staples

Greek yogurt, sourdough

Storage

In the fridge, where they'll stay crisp for two or three weeks. Handle them gently; they bruise.
