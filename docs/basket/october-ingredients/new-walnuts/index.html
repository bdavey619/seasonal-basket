October · San Diego

New walnuts

California grows almost every walnut sold in the United States, and the harvest runs from September into early November. Walnuts go rancid faster than most nuts, so the difference between this year's and last year's is not subtle: new ones are sweet and a little milky, with none of the bitterness people assume walnuts have.

How to choose it

  • Pale gold halves; dark or gray ones are older
  • No oily, papery, or paint-like smell
  • Taste one if you can — it should be sweet
  • In-shell walnuts are heavy and don't rattle
  • Bulk bins that turn over fast beat bags that have sat

Buy this much: 1 lb of halves. A cup goes into the jar, the rest into everything else, and whatever is left into the freezer.

Cheapest in the fall, right after harvest. In-shell costs less and keeps longer.

During the week

  • Toasted and tossed into pasta with arugula and lemon
  • Over yogurt with dates and pomegranate
  • In the arugula salad
  • Ground into the muhammara

On a weekend

Toasted and scattered over the October salad, still warm.

One thing worth learning

Toast every nut before you use it. It takes three minutes in a dry pan over medium heat, shaking often, and it's done when you can smell it from across the kitchen. It changes a walnut from waxy to crisp and from mild to deep, and it's the step people skip most.

Good with this month

Pomegranates, New-crop dates, Arugula, Asian pears, Kabocha squash

Good with your staples

Greek yogurt, sourdough, pasta, beans

Storage

In the fridge for a few months, or the freezer for a year. Never in the pantry — the oils turn in weeks at room temperature.
