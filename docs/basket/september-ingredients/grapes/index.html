September · San Diego

Table grapes

California table grapes peak from late August through October, and September is when the good ones get cheap. This is the one thing in the basket that asks nothing of you — no technique, no recipe, no heat. Buy them, keep them cold, eat them.

How to choose it

  • Green stems, not brown and brittle — that is the freshness tell, not the fruit
  • Grapes firmly attached; if they fall off the bunch, it is old
  • A dusty white bloom on the skin is a good sign, not dirt
  • Plump, with no shriveling where they meet the stem
  • Taste one if the market allows it. Sweetness does not increase after picking

Buy this much: 1–2 lbs. One pound gets eaten; the second one becomes the drink.

The cheapest weeks of the year for grapes. Prices climb again by late fall when the domestic season ends and imports take over.

During the week

  • Cold, by the handful
  • Halved over yogurt with honey
  • Blended into the agua fresca
  • Frozen on a tray and eaten like candy

On a weekend

On a board with sharp cheese, sourdough, and the apples — twenty minutes of assembly for people who came over.

One thing worth learning

Grapes do not ripen after they are picked. Unlike the peaches and figs of the last two editions, a hard, sour grape stays a hard, sour grape on your counter forever, which is why the stem tells you more than the fruit does. Green, flexible stems mean the bunch came off the vine recently. Brown, dry, brittle stems mean weeks in a cooler, no matter how good the grapes still look.

Good with this month

Julian apples, Limes

Good with your staples

Greek yogurt, sourdough, chicken thighs

Storage

Unwashed, in their bag, in the coldest part of the fridge. Rinse only what you are about to eat; washed grapes turn soft in a day.
