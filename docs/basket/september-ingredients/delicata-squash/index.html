Delicata squash

The first winter squash of the year shows up at the end of September, and delicata is the one worth buying while it is still hot outside. It is smaller, sweeter, and faster than the squash that comes in November, and unlike them, you do not have to peel it.

How to choose it

  • Cream-colored with green stripes — orange in the grooves means longer cured and sweeter
  • Firm all over, with no soft or wet spots at either end
  • Heavy for its size
  • A dry, intact stem still attached
  • Small to medium; the large ones get watery

Buy this much: 2 squash. That is two dinners' worth of roasted half-moons, or one dinner and a week of rice bowl additions.

Usually sold by the piece rather than by weight, which makes the medium ones the better buy. Early-season squash costs a little more than it will in November.

During the week

  • Roasted half-moons over rice
  • Folded into a taco with chile strips and escabeche
  • Roasted on the same sheet pan as chicken thighs
  • Cold from the fridge, on yogurt with salt and lime

On a weekend

A whole tray roasted dark alongside chicken, with the escabeche jar on the table. Almost no work, and it makes the kitchen smell like the season changed.

One thing worth learning

Winter squash is sweeter than it looks, and the way to find that out is heat and space. Cut it thin, oil it well, spread it out with room between the pieces, and roast it at 425°F or higher until the edges go genuinely dark — not golden, dark. Crowd the pan and it steams into something soft and bland. It is the same lesson eggplant taught in August, on a different vegetable.

Good with this month

Roasting chiles, Cilantro, Julian apples, Limes

Good with your staples

sticky white rice, chicken thighs, beans, Greek yogurt, ground beef or turkey

Storage

On the counter, out of the sun, for a month — it does not need the fridge and does better without it. Once cut, wrap and refrigerate, and use within four days.
