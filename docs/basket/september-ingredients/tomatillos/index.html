Tomatillos

Tomatillos run from midsummer into October and are at their best right now — heavy, tight in the husk, and tart in a way that no other ingredient in the basket is. They are also the cheapest sauce you can make from scratch.

How to choose it

  • The husk should be tight and papery, not shriveled or loose
  • The fruit should fill its husk completely
  • Firm, with a slight tackiness underneath — that is normal, rinse it off
  • Bright green, not yellowing; yellow means sweeter and duller
  • Small ones are usually tarter than large ones

Buy this much: 1 lb. That is one batch of salsa verde, which is about a week's worth.

Inexpensive year-round and especially now. A pound of tomatillos makes more salsa than a jar of it costs.

During the week

  • Salsa verde for tacos
  • A pot of salsa verde for simmering chicken
  • Eggs poached in salsa verde
  • Spooned over a rice bowl

On a weekend

As salsa verde, on the table with the chile-rubbed fish.

One thing worth learning

Tomatillos are not unripe tomatoes. They are a different plant, related to the ground cherry, and the sourness is structural rather than a sign that you got there too early. That means you cannot fix a tomatillo by waiting. What you can do is decide how much of that sharpness you want: raw keeps all of it, boiling rounds it off, charring trades some of it for smoke.

Good with this month

Roasting chiles, Cilantro, Limes

Good with your staples

eggs, rice, black beans

Storage

Leave the husks on and keep them in a paper bag in the crisper — two weeks, easily. Husked, they go sticky and soft faster. Blended salsa keeps five days.
