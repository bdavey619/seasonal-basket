September · San Diego

Julian apples

Julian is an hour inland and four thousand feet up, and its apples come down the mountain starting in September. A local apple picked this week and eaten raw is a completely different fruit from one that has been in cold storage since last fall — crisper, sharper, and more fragrant.

How to choose it

  • Firm all over, with no give when you press near the stem
  • Smell the blossom end — a fresh apple smells like an apple
  • Skin should be taut, not waxy or slippery
  • Small bruises are fine; soft brown patches are not
  • Ask what came in this week rather than choosing by variety

Buy this much: 4–5 for the week, and buy more than that if you actually drive up. They keep.

Mountain apples cost more than the bin at the grocery store, and for the first few weeks of the season they are worth it. By November, the difference narrows.

During the week

  • Grated over yogurt with honey
  • Shredded into a quick lime-and-cilantro slaw for tacos
  • Sliced with cheddar and sourdough for lunch
  • Eaten whole, cold, in the afternoon

On a weekend

A slaw of shredded apple, cabbage, lime, salt, and a lot of cilantro, on the table next to anything you charred.

One thing worth learning

Apples are one of the few things in the produce aisle where nothing is truly out of season — but almost everything is out of harvest. Most of the year you are eating fruit that has been held in controlled cold storage for months, which is impressive and also why supermarket apples taste the same in March as in December. September and October are the weeks when you can taste the difference, and the only way to notice it is to eat one raw.

Good with this month

Delicata squash, Table grapes, Limes, Cilantro

Good with your staples

Greek yogurt, sourdough, chicken thighs, ground beef or turkey

Storage

Refrigerate them. A cold apple keeps for a month and stays crisp; a counter apple goes mealy in a week. Keep them away from anything that absorbs smells.
