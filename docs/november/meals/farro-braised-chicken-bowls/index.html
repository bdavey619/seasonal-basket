Farro and braised chicken bowls

What the weekend braise turns into midweek: nutty, chewy farro, the chicken pulled off the bone and warmed in its leeks and juices, chard wilted in, and the jar on top.

35 minutes, with leftover braise · Dinner tonight and a lunch

About 1,050 kcal and 70 g protein a serving

You'll need

  • ¾ cup farro, whole or semi-pearled
  • 4 leftover braised chicken thighs, with their leeks and juices
  • ½ bunch Swiss chard, stems chopped, leaves torn
  • Pickled fennel, a big forkful
  • ½ lemon
  • Grated Parmesan
  • Salt and black pepper

Make it

Tap a step when it's done.

  1. 25–30 minSimmer the farro in a big pot of salted water, like pasta, until tender with a little chew. Pearled farro is faster — start checking at 15 minutes. Drain.
  2. Meanwhile, pull the chicken off the bones in big pieces and discard the bones. Keep the skin if you like it.
  3. 5 minWarm the leeks and juices from the braise in a skillet over medium. Add the chard stems and cook until tender, then the chicken and the chard leaves until the leaves wilt and everything is hot.
  4. FinishFarro in bowls, the chicken and greens over it with plenty of juice, pickled fennel, Parmesan, black pepper, and lemon.

Leftovers

Cooked farro keeps four days in the fridge; make a double batch and use it under the flat iron or the soup.

How it works

Braise once, eat twice: the juices left in the pot are the best sauce of the week, and farro is the grain built to soak them up without going soft. Farro is a whole grain when it's whole or semi-pearled; pearled farro cooks faster because most of the bran has been polished off.

Make it your own

  • Rice instead of farro, if that's what's in the pantry.
  • Leftover roast chicken thighs from the cauliflower pan.
  • A fried egg on top.

Checked against

  • How to prepare farro — Food52 — pearled cooks faster; semi-pearled and whole take longer
