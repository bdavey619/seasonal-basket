Minestrone with sausage

The Italian fall soup, made from November's basket: leeks, fennel, chard, white beans, a Parmesan rind for depth, and Italian sausage browned first so the whole pot tastes of it. The quicker of the month's two soups.

About 1 hour · About six big bowls

About 550 kcal and 30 g protein a serving

You'll need

  • ¾ lb Italian sausage, casings removed
  • 2 leeks, sliced and rinsed
  • 1 fennel bulb, diced
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 1 bunch Swiss chard, stems chopped, leaves torn
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 can (28 oz) whole tomatoes, crushed by hand
  • 2 cans white beans, drained
  • 1 carton (4 cups) chicken stock, plus 3 cups water
  • A Parmesan rind, and grated Parmesan to serve
  • 1 cup small pasta (ditalini, or orecchiette)
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt, black pepper, and chile flakes

Make it

Tap a step when it's done.

  1. 6–8 minHeat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high. Brown the sausage, breaking it up, and lift it out with a slotted spoon.
  2. 8–10 minLower the heat to medium. Add the leeks, fennel, carrots, chard stems, and a pinch of salt and cook in the sausage fat until soft. Add the garlic and a pinch of chile flakes for the last minute.
  3. Add the tomatoes and their juice, the beans, stock, water, Parmesan rind, and the sausage, and bring to a simmer.
  4. 25 minSimmer gently, partly covered, until the vegetables are tender and the broth tastes rich.
  5. 10 minAdd the pasta and cook until tender. If you're keeping most of the pot for the week, cook the pasta separately and add it to each bowl, so it doesn't swell in the fridge.
  6. 2 minStir in the chard leaves until they wilt. Take out the rind and season with salt and pepper.
  7. To serveGrated Parmesan, black pepper, and a thread of olive oil on each bowl. Cool the rest quickly and refrigerate it within 2 hours.

Leftovers

It keeps four days in the fridge and three months in the freezer (without the pasta). On day two, it's even better; on day three, it's the start of ribollita.

Make it a meal

Toast a thick slice of sourdough with Parmesan, and crack an egg into the simmering bowl, covered, for four minutes.

How it works

Minestrone is a method more than a recipe: soften what's in season in fat, add tomatoes, beans, and liquid, simmer until it tastes rich, then add pasta and greens at the end. Browning the sausage first seasons everything after it, and the Parmesan rind gives the broth a depth that water and stock alone don't.

Make it your own

  • Leave out the sausage and start with more olive oil, for a meat-free pot.
  • Cauliflower or a diced sweet potato with the carrots.
  • Shredded leftover braised chicken stirred in at the end.

Checked against
