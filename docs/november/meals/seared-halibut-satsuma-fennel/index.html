Seared halibut, fennel and satsuma

California halibut is in season now. Sear it the way October taught — dry fish, hot pan, hands off — and serve it beside a cold salad of fennel shaved paper-thin and satsuma rounds, dressed with lemon, olive oil, and a pinch of chile.

25 minutes · Dinner tonight and a lunch

About 700 kcal and 50 g protein a serving

You'll need

  • 1 lb halibut (or lingcod, or rockfish), about 1 inch thick, in two pieces
  • 1 fennel bulb, shaved thin (keep the fronds)
  • 3 satsumas, peeled and sliced into rounds
  • ½ lemon
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt, black pepper, and chile flakes
  • Rice: about 1 cup cooked per serving

Make it

Tap a step when it's done.

  1. 10 minSalt the fish and leave it on a plate. Pat it completely dry right before it goes in the pan.
  2. Shave the fennel with the food processor's thin slicing disk, or as thin as you can with a sharp knife. Toss it with the lemon juice, 2 tbsp olive oil, and salt, and lay the satsuma rounds over it with the fronds and a pinch of chile flakes.
  3. 4–5 minHeat 1 tbsp oil in the nonstick or cast-iron skillet over medium-high until it shimmers. Lay the fish in, away from you, and leave it until the underside is golden and it releases on its own.
  4. 3–5 minFlip and cook until the thickest part flakes when you press it (the USDA's safe temperature is 145°F). Thinner fillets take less.
  5. FinishFish over rice, the salad beside it, and the salad's juices spooned over the fish.

Leftovers

Cook both pieces and keep one for lunch: cold, flaked over rice with the rest of the salad. Don't reheat it.

How it works

Lean, sweet fish wants something crisp, cold, and sharp beside it, not a heavy sauce. Halibut is firmer than October's rockfish, so it needs a few more minutes a side, but it's the same sear: the crust forms when you stop moving it. Fennel and citrus is the Sicilian salad; shaved thin, the fennel turns tender in the dressing in a few minutes and stays crunchy.

Make it your own

  • Lingcod or rockfish if the halibut is out; thinner fillets cook faster.
  • A forkful of pickled fennel in place of the raw fennel.
  • The air fryer at 400°F for 8–10 minutes instead of the pan, if you'd rather keep the smell out of the kitchen.
