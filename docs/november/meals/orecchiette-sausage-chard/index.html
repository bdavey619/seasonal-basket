Orecchiette with sausage and chard

The classic way orecchiette is eaten in southern Italy — with sausage and bitter greens — made with November's leeks and chard. The sausage browns first, and its fat melts the leeks.

30 minutes · Dinner tonight and a lunch

About 1,100 kcal and 55 g protein a serving

You'll need

  • 8 oz orecchiette
  • ¾ lb Italian sausage, casings removed
  • 2 leeks, sliced thin and rinsed
  • 1 bunch Swiss chard, stems chopped, leaves torn
  • 1 tbsp butter
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 2 oz Parmesan, grated
  • ½ lemon
  • Salt, black pepper, and chile flakes

Make it

Tap a step when it's done.

  1. Bring a big pot of well-salted water to a boil.
  2. 6–8 minBrown the sausage in a large skillet over medium-high, breaking it into bite-sized pieces, until it's browned and cooked through. Lift it out with a slotted spoon and pour off all but about a tablespoon of the fat.
  3. 8–10 minLower the heat to medium-low, add the butter, leeks, and a pinch of salt, cover, and cook, stirring now and then, until the leeks are soft and sweet.
  4. 5 minUncover, add the chard stems, garlic, and a good pinch of chile flakes, and cook until the stems are tender.
  5. About 11 minBoil the orecchiette until just tender. Before you drain it, scoop out a cup of the pasta water.
  6. 2 minStir the chard leaves into the skillet until wilted. Add the pasta, the sausage, half the Parmesan, and a splash of pasta water, and toss over the heat until glossy.
  7. FinishThe rest of the Parmesan, black pepper, and a squeeze of lemon.

Leftovers

Pack a bowl for lunch. It's best warmed in a skillet with a splash of water.

How it works

Sausage seasons the whole dish: a spoonful of its fat cooks the leeks, and the bits stuck to the pan dissolve into the sauce. Leeks cooked low and covered don't brown; they melt. Starchy pasta water and Parmesan, tossed over the heat, pull it all together without cream. Chard stems go in before the leaves because they need longer.

Make it your own

  • A can of white beans instead of the sausage, for a meat-free night.
  • Ground turkey with a pinch of chile and garlic, if you'd rather skip pork.
  • Shredded leftover braised chicken instead of the sausage.
