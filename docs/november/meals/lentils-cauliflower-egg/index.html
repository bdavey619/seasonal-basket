Warm lentils and cauliflower

French green lentils simmered until just tender and dressed while they're warm with the jar's brine, folded through dark-roasted cauliflower and pickled fennel, with a runny egg on top.

45 minutes · Dinner tonight and a lunch

About 900 kcal and 50 g protein a serving

You'll need

  • 1 cup French green lentils, rinsed
  • 1 head cauliflower, in small florets
  • 4 eggs — two per serving
  • Pickled fennel, a big forkful, plus 3 tbsp of its brine
  • 1 garlic clove, grated
  • 4 tbsp olive oil, plus a little for the eggs
  • Grated Parmesan
  • Salt, black pepper, and chile flakes

Make it

Tap a step when it's done.

  1. 425°F · 30 minHeat the oven. Toss the cauliflower with 2 tbsp oil and salt, spread it out on a sheet pan, and roast, stirring once, until golden at the edges.
  2. 20–25 minMeanwhile, cover the lentils with a few inches of water in a pot, add a pinch of salt, and simmer until tender but still holding their shape. Drain.
  3. While they're warm, toss the lentils with the brine, 2 tbsp olive oil, the garlic, salt, pepper, and a pinch of chile flakes. Fold in the cauliflower and the pickled fennel.
  4. 3 minFry the eggs in a little olive oil until the whites are crisp at the edges and the yolks are runny.
  5. FinishLentils in a bowl, two eggs on top, grated Parmesan and black pepper over everything.

Leftovers

The lentils and cauliflower keep three days. For lunch, eat them at room temperature with a squeeze of lemon, and fry fresh eggs if you can.

How it works

Lentils are earthy and plain, so they want sharpness and fat — and like pasta, they take in a dressing best while they're warm. The jar's brine is the vinegar, and the egg yolk is the richness. French green lentils hold their shape where brown ones turn soft.

Make it your own

  • Leftover pulled chicken thigh instead of the eggs.
  • Seared halibut, flaked over the top.
  • Melted leeks folded in with the cauliflower.
