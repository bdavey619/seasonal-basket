Seared halibut, persimmon salad

California halibut is in season now. Sear it the way October taught — dry fish, hot pan, hands off — and serve it beside a cold salad of Fuyu persimmon and fennel, shaved thin with lemon and Parmesan.

25 minutes · Dinner tonight and a lunch

About 800 kcal and 55 g protein a serving

You'll need

  • 1 lb halibut (or lingcod, or rockfish), about 1 inch thick, in two pieces
  • 2 Fuyu persimmons, sliced thin
  • 1 fennel bulb, shaved thin (keep the fronds)
  • 1 lemon
  • 1 oz Parmesan, shaved with a peeler
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt and black pepper
  • Rice: about 1 cup cooked per serving

Make it

Tap a step when it's done.

  1. 10 minSalt the fish and leave it on a plate. Pat it completely dry right before it goes in the pan.
  2. Toss the fennel and persimmon with the juice of half the lemon, 2 tbsp olive oil, salt, and pepper. Scatter the Parmesan and fronds over the top.
  3. 4–5 minHeat 1 tbsp oil in a nonstick or well-seasoned skillet over medium-high until it shimmers. Lay the fish in, away from you, and leave it until the underside is golden and it releases on its own.
  4. 3–5 minFlip and cook until the thickest part flakes when you press it (the USDA's safe temperature is 145°F). Thinner fillets take less.
  5. FinishFish over rice, the salad beside it, the rest of the lemon squeezed over everything.

Leftovers

Cook both pieces and keep one for lunch: cold, flaked over rice with the rest of the salad. Don't reheat it.

How it works

Lean, sweet fish wants something crisp and sweet-sharp beside it, not a heavy sauce. Halibut is firmer than October's rockfish, so it needs a few more minutes a side, but it's the same sear: the crust forms when you stop moving it. Fuyu persimmon is the rare fruit that's crisp enough to shave like a vegetable.

Make it your own

  • Lingcod or rockfish if the halibut is out; thinner fillets cook faster.
  • A forkful of pickled fennel in place of the raw fennel.
  • Satsuma segments in the salad instead of persimmon.

Checked against

  • Pan-Seared Halibut with Wilted Bitter Salad — America's Test Kitchen — inch-thick fillets, medium-high heat, about 8–10 minutes in all, flipped once
  • Fennel Slaw with Persimmons — Food52 — shaved fennel with firm Fuyu persimmon, so the slices hold their shape
  • Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart — USDA — fish is done at 145°F, or when it's opaque and flakes with a fork
