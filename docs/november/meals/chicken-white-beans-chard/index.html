One-pot chicken and white beans

A weeknight braise in the Dutch oven: thighs seared until the skin is deep gold, then finished uncovered over white beans, chard, and satsuma, so the skin stays crisp above the pot and the meat stays juicy in it. The beans soak up everything.

About 55 minutes, most of it in the oven · Dinner tonight and a lunch

About 1,000 kcal and 70 g protein a serving

You'll need

  • 4 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (about 1¾ lbs)
  • 1 can white beans, drained
  • ½ bunch Swiss chard, stems chopped, leaves torn
  • 1 cup chicken stock
  • 1 satsuma — zest and juice
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Pickled fennel, to serve
  • Sourdough, to serve
  • Salt, black pepper, and chile flakes

Make it

Tap a step when it's done.

  1. 350°FHeat the oven with a rack in the upper-middle position. Pat the chicken completely dry and salt both sides.
  2. 8–10 minHeat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high. Lay the thighs in skin-side down and leave them alone until the skin is deep golden and releases on its own. Move them to a plate, skin-side up.
  3. 3 minPour off all but a tablespoon of fat. Add the chard stems, garlic, and a pinch of chile flakes and cook until the stems soften.
  4. Add the beans, stock, and satsuma juice, scrape the bottom of the pot, and bring it to a simmer.
  5. 35–40 minNestle the thighs into the beans skin-side up, with the skin above the liquid. Slide the pot into the oven uncovered and cook until the thickest thigh reads about 185°F; the liquid reduces and the skin crisps in the top heat.
  6. 2 minMove the chicken to a plate. Stir the chard leaves into the hot beans until they wilt, and season with salt and pepper.
  7. FinishBeans and greens in shallow bowls, a thigh or two on top, satsuma zest, pickled fennel, and bread for the juices.

Leftovers

Pack the second serving before you sit down: a thigh over the beans with plenty of juice. It reheats well, covered, in a low oven.

How it works

A short braise gives you crisp skin and juicy meat at once: the skin renders and crisps in the pot first, then sits above the liquid in a top-heated oven, while the meat below stays in the liquid and can't dry out. Thighs are best around 185°F, where their connective tissue has melted. The beans and stock become the sauce.

Make it your own

  • Cauliflower florets with the beans, in place of the chard.
  • A lemon instead of the satsuma, for a sharper pot.
  • French green lentils (cooked) instead of the beans.
