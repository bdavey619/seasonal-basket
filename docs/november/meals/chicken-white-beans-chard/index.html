Crisp chicken and white beans

Bone-in thighs in the air fryer, skin-side up, until the skin crackles and the meat is juicy, set over white beans simmered in a skillet with garlic, chard, stock, and satsuma. A spoonful of the chicken's drippings goes into the beans.

About 40 minutes · Dinner tonight and a lunch

About 950 kcal and 70 g protein a serving

You'll need

  • 4 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (about 1¾ lbs)
  • 1 can white beans, drained and rinsed
  • ½ bunch Swiss chard, stems chopped, leaves torn
  • ½ cup chicken stock
  • 1 satsuma — zest and juice
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Pickled fennel, to serve
  • Sourdough, to serve
  • Salt, black pepper, and chile flakes

Make it

Tap a step when it's done.

  1. Pat the chicken completely dry, trim any skin hanging off the edges, and salt both sides. Dry skin is what crisps.
  2. 375°F · 25–30 minSet the thighs in the air fryer skin-side up, spaced apart in one layer. Cook until the skin is deep golden and crisp and the thickest thigh reads about 185°F. There's no need to flip them.
  3. 3 minWith 15 minutes to go, warm the oil in the 12-inch skillet over medium. Add the garlic, chard stems, and a pinch of chile flakes and cook until the stems soften.
  4. 5 minAdd the beans, stock, and satsuma juice and simmer, mashing a few beans against the pan so the liquid turns creamy.
  5. 2 minStir in the chard leaves until they wilt, and a spoonful of the drippings from under the chicken. Season with salt and pepper.
  6. FinishLet the chicken rest for 5 minutes. Beans and greens in shallow bowls, a thigh or two on top, satsuma zest, pickled fennel, and bread for the juices.

Leftovers

Pack a thigh over the beans for lunch. Recrisp the chicken in the air fryer at 375°F for 5 minutes and warm the beans in a small pan.

How it works

An air fryer is a small, powerful convection oven: hot air moving fast over the skin dries and renders it, and the fat drips away instead of pooling, so the skin crisps without a sear. Thighs are forgiving and are best around 185°F, where their connective tissue has melted. Beans simmered with stock and mashed a little make their own sauce, and the drippings give it the chicken's flavor.

Make it your own

  • Cauliflower florets in the beans, in place of the chard.
  • A lemon instead of the satsuma, for sharper beans.
  • French green lentils (cooked) instead of the beans.
