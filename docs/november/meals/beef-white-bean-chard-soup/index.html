Beef and white bean soup

November's first soup, and the week's make-ahead: beef chuck browned hard and simmered until it falls apart, with leeks, white beans, and a whole bunch of chard. Made on Sunday, it's dinner twice and lunch through the week, with a few bowls for the freezer.

About 2½ hours, almost all of it simmering · About six big bowls

About 650 kcal and 50 g protein a serving

You'll need

  • 2½ lbs boneless beef chuck roast, in 1½-inch pieces
  • 2 leeks, sliced and rinsed
  • 1 bunch Swiss chard, stems chopped, leaves torn
  • 2 cans white beans, drained
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 carton (4 cups) chicken stock, plus 4 cups water
  • A Parmesan rind, if your wedge has one, and grated Parmesan to serve
  • 1 lemon
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt, black pepper, and chile flakes

Make it

Tap a step when it's done.

  1. Pat the beef dry and salt it well.
  2. 12–15 minHeat the oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high. Brown the beef in two or three batches, without crowding, until it's dark on at least two sides. Move each batch to a bowl.
  3. 6–8 minLower the heat to medium. Add the leeks, chard stems, and a pinch of salt and cook until soft. Add the garlic and a pinch of chile flakes for the last minute.
  4. Pour in the stock and water and scrape the bottom clean. Return the beef and its juices, add the Parmesan rind, and bring to a simmer.
  5. 1½–2 hrsCover with the lid slightly ajar and keep it at a gentle simmer — a few lazy bubbles, never a boil — until the beef breaks apart when pressed with a spoon.
  6. 20 minAdd the beans and simmer uncovered so the broth thickens a little.
  7. 5 minStir in the chard leaves until they wilt. Take out the rind, squeeze in half the lemon, and season with salt and pepper.
  8. To serveGrated Parmesan, black pepper, and a forkful of pickled fennel on top. Cool the rest quickly, refrigerate it within 2 hours, and freeze what you won't eat in four days.

Leftovers

It keeps four days in the fridge and three months in the freezer, and it's better on day two. Reheat a bowl until it's steaming, and add a little water if it has thickened.

How it works

Chuck is a hard-working shoulder muscle full of connective tissue; a hot pan makes it tough, but two hours at a gentle simmer melts that tissue into the broth, which is why the meat goes soft and the soup tastes rich. Browning first is what makes it taste like beef rather than boiled meat. Beans and chard go in late, so they hold their shape.

Make it your own

  • Bone-in chicken thighs instead of beef; they're tender in about 45 minutes.
  • Cauliflower florets with the beans for a thicker soup.
  • A spoonful of melted leeks on top instead of the lemon.
