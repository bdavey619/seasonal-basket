November

Keep the meal. Change the season.

November is when San Diego's evenings finally turn. The days still reach the high sixties, but the nights drop into the fifties, and for the first time since spring an hour with a pot in the oven sounds good. The first satsumas arrive, and the cool-weather vegetables come back — leeks, fennel, chard, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts. The month cooks at two speeds: one slow pot and one soup for the weekend, and quick, crisp, bright cooking the rest of the week, from the air fryer and the wok. Everything below comes from one store and a fish market.

Mornings, and in between

Small things from the same basket: quick on weekdays, slower on the weekend.

Weekdays
  • Satsuma and yogurt. Thick yogurt, a peeled satsuma in segments, its zest, a pinch of salt, and a thread of olive oil.
  • Soup and an egg. A mug of the week's soup brought to a simmer, an egg cracked in, the lid on for four minutes. Breakfast that's already made.
  • Tomatoes and eggs. China's home classic, in ten minutes: three eggs scrambled soft in the wok and set aside, two chopped tomatoes cooked with garlic, salt, and a pinch of sugar until saucy, the eggs folded back in. Over rice or toast. In a lomo saltado week, buy two extra Roma tomatoes for it.
Weekends
  • Chard and Parmesan frittata. Chard stems softened in butter, the leaves wilted in, eight eggs and plenty of Parmesan, finished in the oven.
  • Eggs in the braise. The morning after: warm the leftover leeks and juices from the braise, make wells, crack in eggs, and cover until the whites set. Toast for dragging.
  • Fried rice. The week's leftover rice in a hot wok with garlic, two eggs, a splash of fish sauce, and the last of the basil.
In between
  • Satsumas. Three, peeled at your desk. The reason to buy a bag.
  • Toast and the jar. Sourdough, a little butter, pickled fennel, and shaved Parmesan.
  • A cup of the soup. Hot, with a forkful of the pickled fennel on top.

November's four flavors
  • Bright and sharp Pickled fennel + its brine A forkful of the jar beside anything rich, and a spoonful of brine wherever you'd use vinegar.
  • Salty and sharp Parmesan + lemon + black pepper Grate it fine, zest the lemon over, and crack pepper on top. Finishes soup, pasta, and greens.
  • Salty and hot Fish sauce + sliced chiles Thailand's table sauce: two tablespoons of fish sauce with a sliced chile. A spoonful over rice, eggs, or anything from the wok.
  • Crisp and sweet Satsuma + shaved fennel + lemon Slice both thin and dress them with lemon, olive oil, and salt. A cold salad beside anything warm.

One thing to notice

A braise should barely move.

Open the oven and look at the pot. The liquid around the chicken should be shivering — a few lazy bubbles at the edges — not boiling. A hard boil tightens the meat and clouds the sauce. If it's rolling, turn the oven down twenty-five degrees. The same goes for the soup on the stove.
