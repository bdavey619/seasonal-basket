October

Keep the meal. Change the season.

October is San Diego's second summer. Some years the hottest week lands now, when the wind blows in from the desert. But the harvest has moved inland — new dates, new sweet potatoes, heavy pomegranates, the last sweet peppers — and as the water cools, the local rockfish comes in. Everything below comes from one store and a fish market, and it all keeps you cooking through the week.

Mornings, and in between

Small things from the same basket: quick on weekdays, slower on the weekend.

Weekdays
  • Eggs and the jar. Boiled eggs, halved and salted, with a spoonful of ajvar and a cold wedge of last night's kabocha. Ready before the kettle.
  • Yogurt, pomegranate, date. Thick yogurt with a pinch of salt and a thread of olive oil, pomegranate seeds, and a torn date. The October plate, at breakfast.
  • Sweet potato toast. A leftover baked sweet potato, smashed warm onto sourdough, with a fried egg, chile flakes, and lime.
Weekends
  • Eggs on garlic yogurt. The Turkish way: soft-poached eggs on salted garlic yogurt, chile flakes sizzled in brown butter poured over, sourdough to drag through it.
  • Slow eggs, feta, and the jar. Three eggs scrambled slowly in butter, feta folded in at the end, on olive-oil toast with ajvar alongside.
  • Squash hash. Cubes of roasted kabocha or sweet potato crisped hard in a skillet with any leftover pork or sausage and a handful of torn kale, eggs cracked into the gaps, lime over the top.
In between
  • Dates and feta. A date split open, a piece of feta tucked inside, a pinch of chile. Sweet, salty, done.
  • Pomegranate, lime, chile. Seeds in a cup with lime, salt, and chile flakes — the way San Diego's fruit carts sell everything.
  • A bowl of the orzo. From the fridge, with a handful of arugula and a squeeze of lemon.

October's four flavors
  • Smoky Ajvar + lemon A spoonful of the jar and its oil, lemon squeezed over everything.
  • Cool and sharp Salted yogurt + pomegranate Yogurt on the plate first, the hot food on top, seeds over it all.
  • Bright and hot Chile-garlic oil + lime Warm olive oil with a grated garlic clove and chile flakes for one minute. Pour it over; squeeze lime hard.

One thing to notice

A fresh fillet is glossy.

At the fish counter, look before you ask. Fresh fish is glossy and slightly translucent, with no gaps opening between the layers of flesh. Dull, dry, or separating means it's been sitting. Ask to smell it; it should smell like the ocean and nothing else. It takes five seconds and tells you more than any label.
