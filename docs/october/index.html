October

Keep the meal. Change the season.

October in San Diego doesn't look like fall. Some of the year's hottest days land this month. But the evenings shorten, the squash is fully cured, the last poblanos are drying into anchos, and the pomegranates are heavy. It's the first month worth turning the oven on for an hour.

This month tastes like
Squash · Pomegranate · Ancho
Warm afternoons and the first slow dinners.

San Diego ritual

Walk out for the lobster opener.

Lobster season opens at dusk on the first weekend of October, and the piers on Shelter Island fill with hoop nets and headlamps. You don't need to fish. Walk out after dark with something warm, watch the first nets come up, and notice that you wanted the jacket — the first night of the year that's true.

One thing to notice

The corners on a pomegranate.

An underripe pomegranate is round. As the seeds swell, they press out against the skin until the fruit goes slightly squared-off, with flat faces and soft ridges. The angular ones are the full ones. Once you see it, you'll stop picking the pretty round one.
