October

Keep the meal. Change the season.

October in San Diego doesn't look like fall; some of the year's hottest days land this month. But the evenings shorten, the squash gets sweeter, the last poblanos are going, and at some point you'll want the oven on. This edition is about that moment.

Pairings

What to cook it with.

Meat isn't in season the way squash is. These are the proteins October's basket points toward — and one from the water that genuinely is.

Bone-in chicken thighsBuy for the week

Boneless thighs are for the skillet; bone-in, skin-on are for the pot. The bone gives the liquid body, and the skin renders into the fat everything else cooks in.

With Delicata squash · The last poblanos · White beans · Rosemary · Lemon

Skin down. Leave it alone. Pat dry → salt → skin down in a heavy pan → 12 minutes untouched → flip → finish at 425°F

Thick-cut pork chopsBuy for the week

Apples, sage, and pork: the oldest fall pairing, and it holds up. Buy them an inch thick — thin chops overcook before they brown.

With Late Julian apples · Sweet potatoes · Sage · Cider vinegar

Hot pan. Don't move it. Salt an hour ahead → very hot pan → 4 minutes a side → rest 5 → apples into the same pan

California spiny lobsterIf you see it

The one protein here that is literally in season: it opens off San Diego the first weekend of October. Eat it the way it's eaten an hour south, with beans, rice, and flour tortillas.

With Beans · Rice · Flour tortillas · Lime · The last poblanos

Split it. Broil it. Split lengthwise → butter + garlic → flesh up under the broiler → 6–8 minutes → lime

The pantry that unlocks it
  • White beans Two cans turn a pan of chicken into dinner for four.
  • Stock or bouillon The braising liquid. Bouillon paste is fine; nobody will know.
  • Rosemary and sage Hardy herbs go in early and cook. Summer's soft herbs went in last.
  • Lemon The last step of a braise. Without it, the pot tastes heavy.
  • Cider vinegar The pan sauce for pork, and the dressing for anything sweet.
Method

How October cooks.

Two patterns. One is new; one you already have. Both keep working long after October.

BraiseNew this month

Brown something, then cook it slowly in a little liquid until it gives. The pot does most of the work.

  1. Sear Brown the meat hard. Take it out.
  2. Aromatics Garlic, chile, herbs, in the fat left behind.
  3. Deglaze A splash of liquid. Scrape up the brown bits.
  4. Liquid Halfway up the meat, never over it.
  5. Slow cook Low oven, most of an hour.
  6. Brighten Lemon or vinegar at the end. Always.

Stop after the first step and you have a pork chop. The sear is a method on its own.

The Dutch oven.

Cooler evenings, winter squash, and cuts with the bone still in: the heavy pot earns its place back on the stove. Burner to oven to table, and it holds heat so evenly a braise more or less cooks itself.

  • The Sunday braise
  • A pot of beans from dry

No Dutch oven? Any heavy, lidded, oven-safe pot will do.
