October

Keep the meal. Change the season.

October is San Diego's second summer. Some years the hottest week lands now, when the wind turns and blows in from the desert. But the harvest has moved inland: new dates, new walnuts, heavy pomegranates, and a squash that will sit on your counter until Thanksgiving. For the first time since spring, you can shop for the month instead of the week.

San Diego ritual

Go to the desert.

Anza-Borrego is two hours east and unbearable until now. October is the first month it isn't. Go for a day, walk until it gets hot, and notice that the dates, the pomegranates, and the citrus coming next all grow somewhere that looks like this — dry, bright, and irrigated. The season in this basket comes from inland, not from the coast.

One thing to notice

The wind from the wrong direction.

A few times each fall the wind turns around and blows from the desert instead of the ocean. The air goes warm and very dry, the sky turns unusually clear, and from the coast you can suddenly see the mountains. It's why October can be hotter than August. When it happens, look east.
