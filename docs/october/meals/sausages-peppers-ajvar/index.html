Sausages, peppers, and ajvar

Sausages roasted on one pan with squash and sweet peppers until everything browns in their fat, with lacinato kale crisping at the edges in the last minutes — served the Balkan way, with a pile of ajvar on the side and bread to drag through it.

45 minutes, most of it in the oven · Dinner tonight and a lunch

About 1,150 kcal and 55 g protein a serving

You'll need

  • 1 lb chicken sausage, 4–5 links
  • ½ large kabocha (about 1½ lbs), in 1-inch wedges
  • 2 red bell peppers, in thick strips
  • ½ bunch lacinato kale, leaves stripped from the ribs and torn
  • Ajvar, a few big spoonfuls
  • 1 lemon
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt
  • Sourdough or rice: a thick slice, or 1 cup cooked, per serving

Make it

Tap a step when it's done.

  1. 425°FHeat the oven. If the kabocha is too hard to cut, put it in whole for 10 minutes first.
  2. 10 minToss the squash with half the olive oil and a pinch of salt, spread it on a sheet pan, and give it 10 minutes on its own.
  3. Toss the peppers with the rest of the oil and salt and add them to the pan with the sausages, leaving room between everything.
  4. 25–30 minRoast, turning the sausages once, until they're browned and the squash edges are dark. Raw chicken sausage is done at 165°F; precooked just needs to brown.
  5. Last 8–10 minToss the torn kale leaves with 1 tsp olive oil and a pinch of salt and scatter them over and between everything, in one layer where you can. (The thick ribs stay tough in the time the leaves take, which is why they come off.) The kale is done when the edges crisp and the rest has just wilted.
  6. Last 5 minToast the sourdough in a skillet with a little oil while the pan finishes.
  7. FinishEverything on the plate, a big spoonful of ajvar beside it, lemon squeezed over the vegetables.

Leftovers

Pack a lunch before you sit down: sliced sausage, squash, and peppers over 1 cup of rice, ajvar on the side.

How it works

Sausage brings its own fat, and on a hot sheet pan that fat runs out and roasts the vegetables around it — the pan seasons itself. Ajvar is what's served with grilled sausages across the Balkans: smoky, sweet, a little sharp, exactly what cuts the richness. The kale goes in last: on a hot pan it crisps in that same fat in minutes, and a slightly bitter green is what rich sausage wants beside it.

Make it your own

  • Any sausage works — pork or chicken.
  • Sweet potato instead of kabocha, same timing.
  • No ajvar made yet? Lemon, olive oil, and a pinch of chile flakes over the top.
