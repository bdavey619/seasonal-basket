Muhammara

One jar. Charred peppers, new walnuts, and pomegranate, made thick enough to spread.

Make one jar

  • 2 red bell peppers, charred, peeled, and seeded — or one 12-oz jar of roasted red peppers, drained
  • 1 cup new walnuts, toasted
  • ½ cup breadcrumbs, or one slice of stale sourdough, torn
  • 1 garlic clove
  • 2 tbsp pomegranate molasses — or ½ cup pomegranate juice, simmered down to 2 tbsp
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp Aleppo pepper, or ½ tsp red pepper flakes
  • ½ tsp kosher salt
  • ⅓ cup olive oil

Toast the walnuts in a dry pan over medium heat, shaking often, until they smell toasty — about three minutes. Let them cool. Put everything except the olive oil in a food processor and pulse until it's a coarse paste. With the machine running, pour in the oil. Stop while there's still some texture; it should look like a thick, rough spread, not a smooth dip. Taste it: it should be sweet, sour, smoky, and a little hot, all at once. If it's flat, it wants more pomegranate molasses or salt. Charring the peppers is the same dry-flame move from July and August — October just adds nuts and puts it in a jar.

Keeps a week in the refrigerator, with a thin layer of olive oil poured over the top. It thickens cold — let it sit out a few minutes, or loosen it with a spoonful of water.

Use it this week

Rice bowl A big spoonful on top.

It's a sauce and a vegetable at once. Roasted squash and arugula finish the bowl.

Tacos Under everything else.

A smear on the warm tortilla first, then kabocha and beans. It does what crema would, with more going on.

Beans Stir it in.

A spoonful turns a can of beans red, smoky, and finished.

Chicken thighs Spread it on after cooking.

Roasted or grilled thighs with muhammara and arugula. That's dinner.

Toast Spread, then an egg.

Sourdough, muhammara, a fried egg, a few arugula leaves.
