Field Notes

Small things worth knowing this month.

A new date isn't dried fruit.

Most dates in the store were picked last fall and have been sitting since. The ones picked this fall are softer, glossier, and taste like caramel instead of candy — squeeze the box and they give. If you ever see yellow dates still on the branch, those are Barhis, eaten crunchy and fresh for a few weeks and nowhere else. Buy a branch.

October is when peppers go in jars.

Across the Balkans, autumn is ajvar season: red peppers are at their cheapest, and whole families roast them by the crate and cook them down into a relish that lasts the winter. You don't need a crate. Four peppers and one eggplant make a jar. It's the same impulse behind everything in this basket — buy it while it's here, and make it keep.

Let the oven cut the kabocha.

A kabocha is dense enough to be dangerous with a knife. Put it whole in a 400°F oven for ten minutes, or the microwave for three, and the skin softens enough to cut through safely. Don't peel it. Roasted, the skin goes tender and you eat all of it.

Cook it on the bone.

Fish and meat cooked on the bone are harder to overcook. The bone slows the heat, the skin holds in moisture, and the flesh next to the bone stays juicy. That's why a whole fish at 450°F is more forgiving than a fillet in a pan. Learn it on a fish this month; it's the same idea in a pot of bone-in chicken thighs once the evenings actually cool.
