Field Notes

Small things worth knowing this month.

A new date isn't dried fruit.

Most dates sit in storage for months. This fall's are softer, glossier, and taste like caramel instead of candy — squeeze the container and they give. If they're firm, dry, or have sugar crystals on the skin, they're last year's. The fresh ones are worth looking for in the bin you already shop.

October is when peppers go in jars.

Across the Balkans, autumn is ajvar season: red peppers are at their cheapest, and families roast them by the crate and cook them down into a relish that lasts into winter. You don't need a crate. Four peppers make a jar. It's the same impulse behind everything in this basket — buy it while it's here, and make it keep.

Let the oven cut the kabocha.

A kabocha is dense enough to be dangerous with a knife. Put it whole in a 400°F oven for ten minutes, or the microwave for three, and the skin softens enough to cut through safely. Don't peel it. Roasted, the skin goes tender and you eat all of it.

Dry fish, hot pan, hands off.

Fish sticks when it's wet or when you move it too soon. Pat it dry, salt it, heat the pan until the oil shimmers, lay the fillet down away from you, and leave it alone for three minutes. When it's ready, it lets go of the pan on its own. That's also the first step of every braise — you'll use it on bone-in chicken thighs once the evenings cool.
