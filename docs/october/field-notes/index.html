Field Notes

Small things worth knowing this month.

An ancho is a poblano that waited.

Leave a poblano on the plant until it turns red, dry it in the sun, and it becomes an ancho — wrinkled, nearly black, and smelling faintly of raisins. The chile you charred in September and the one in this month's jar are the same pepper, a few weeks apart. That's the whole season in one ingredient.

Halfway up, never over.

The one rule of a braise: after you brown the meat, the liquid comes about halfway up it, not over the top. What's under the surface goes soft and turns to stew; what's above it stays browned, and the skin crisps again in the oven. Drown it and you've made soup. Every braise you make this winter works the same way.

Seed a pomegranate underwater.

Score around the crown, pull the fruit apart into sections, and hold them in a bowl of cold water while you push the seeds out with your thumbs. The seeds sink. The pith floats. Skim it, drain, and you have a cup of seeds and no red kitchen.

October at the fish counter.

California spiny lobster season opens the first weekend of October, and for the first few weeks it is genuinely local, genuinely live, and worth one splurge. Ask for it split. Broil it with butter and garlic and eat it the way it's eaten an hour south: beans, rice, flour tortillas, lime. If it isn't there, the question from July still works: not 'Do you have salmon?' but 'What came in today?'
