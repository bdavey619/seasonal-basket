Ajvar

One jar. Red peppers, charred and cooked down, the way autumn has always kept them.

Make one jar

  • 4 red bell peppers
  • 1 medium eggplant
  • 2 garlic cloves, grated
  • ⅓ cup olive oil
  • 1 tbsp red wine vinegar or lemon juice
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • ½ tsp sugar
  • Chile flakes, to taste

Char the peppers and the whole eggplant on a gas burner, grill, or under the broiler, turning until they're black all over and the eggplant collapses — the same dry-flame move from July and August. Put the peppers in a covered bowl for fifteen minutes, then peel and seed them. Split the eggplant and scoop out the flesh. Chop both finely by hand, or pulse a few times; it should stay rough, not smooth. Warm the oil in a wide pan over medium-low, add everything else, and cook, stirring often, for 20 to 30 minutes, until the relish thickens, darkens, and the oil starts to separate at the edges. Taste: it should be sweet, smoky, and sharp. Spoon it into a jar and pour a thin layer of oil over the top. The idea underneath is the one worth keeping: char, peel, then cook it down slowly until the water is gone and the flavor is concentrated. That's how a vegetable becomes something that keeps.

Keeps two weeks in the refrigerator under a thin layer of olive oil. Use a clean spoon, and top up the oil when you take some out.

Use it this week

Rice bowl A big spoonful on top.

It's a sauce and a vegetable at once. Roasted squash and arugula finish the bowl.

Tacos Under everything else.

A smear on the warm tortilla first, then kabocha and beans. Smoky where salsa would be sharp.

Beans Stir it in.

A spoonful turns a can of beans red, smoky, and finished.

Chicken thighs Spread it on after cooking.

Roasted or grilled thighs with ajvar and arugula. That's dinner.
