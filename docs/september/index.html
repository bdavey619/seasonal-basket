September

Keep the meal. Change the season.

Two seasons share the table this month. Chiles are at their best and about to be gone. The first squash and the first apples arrive while it is still eighty degrees out. Buy from both sides. September is the only month that lets you.

The meals

Your usual meals, wearing September.

Keep what you already make. Add what's ripe.

Rice bowl → Roasted chile strips, delicata half-moons, cilantro, a spoonful of escabeche. The jar does the work.
  • Chile strips + escabeche + cilantro The version you'll make most often. Three minutes if the jar is in the fridge.
  • Delicata + chile strips + lime Sweet against smoke. The one that feels like fall showed up.
  • Salsa verde + beans + cilantro No protein needed. Sharp, green, and finished.

Pasta → Blended poblano and cream. Cilantro and lime after the heat. Green, and not from basil.
  • Poblano + cream + cilantro The base version. Blend the chiles with the cream and a clove of raw garlic until smooth, then warm it — do not boil it.
  • Poblano + yogurt + lime Lighter and sharper. Take the pan off the heat before the yogurt goes in or it will split.
  • Poblano + cream + delicata Roasted squash folded in at the end. This one eats like a Sunday.

San Diego ritual

Drive up to Julian.

An hour inland and four thousand feet up, the apples come in while the coast is still in shorts. Go on a weekday if you can. Buy more than you meant to, eat one in the car, and notice that it is genuinely cooler up there — the first cool air of the year most San Diegans get.

One thing to notice

The stripes on a delicata.

A delicata's green stripes deepen toward orange as it cures. Pale ones were picked recently and taste closer to summer squash. The ones with real orange down in the grooves have had time, and they're sweeter. Both are worth eating. They're just not the same squash.
